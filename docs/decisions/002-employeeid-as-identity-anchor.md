# ADR 002: EmployeeID Is the Only Automatic Identity Match Key

Author: Alex Scheaffer

Status: Accepted

## Context
The same person existed in HRIS, Active Directory, Entra ID and several SaaS applications, each matched to the others by name or email. Name and email matching broke on name changes, nicknames, duplicate names, rehires and typos. It produced duplicate accounts and, worse, access attached to the wrong person.

## Decision
Use the HRIS-issued **EmployeeID** as the single join key across systems. Store it in AD `employeeID`, sync it to Entra ID `employeeId`, and pass it to SaaS apps through SCIM or SSO claims. Names, email, department and manager are used only to **verify** a match during review, never to make one automatically.

## Rationale
- Issued once by the system of record and never reused or changed
- Unaffected by name changes, preferred names or email changes
- Already present in HRIS, payroll and benefits exports
- Makes every downstream automation (joiner, mover, leaver, email feed, access reviews) deterministic

## Alternatives Considered
- **Email / UPN as the key:** email is derived from the name and changes with it; it's also generated by the directory, not HR.
- **Name + date of birth:** sensitive data spread across more systems, and still not unique.
- **Directory objectGUID:** unique, but HRIS doesn't know it, so it can't join to HR data.

## Consequences
- Existing accounts need a one-time EmployeeID backfill with human verification ([identity-matching.md](../identity-matching.md)).
- Anything without an EmployeeID becomes a Review item rather than being matched on a guess.
- HRIS data quality issues (missing or duplicate IDs) surface immediately and are fixed at the source.
